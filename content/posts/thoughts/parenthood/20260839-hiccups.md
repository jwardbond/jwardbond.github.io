We are getting really excited to meet you, our darling daughter. Our pip-piperoo. Piparoni. Both of us imagine pushing your around through the snow in a stroller; I can see your little pink cheeks, and the pom-pom on your touque. I imagine your little enclosed limbs waving around like some ineffectual octopus unable to grasp anything because your jacket is your pants is your mittens is your booties.

Every day you come a little closer to personhood. A little less _something that's happening to us_ and a little more of a human in your own right. I _think_ we are pretty prepared: The nursery is mostly set up (I really felt like a dad when I built the crib), and we have bought you lots of things to stay snuggly and warm in the winter. We've been going to the midwife, reading the books, signing up for the classes. Still, it feels like there are infinite choices left to make. I was going to list some of them but I think there are actually infinite.

A friend of mine had some good advice for me:

> Don't solve a probem before it's a problem.

Bad advice for work, but pretty reasonable advice for expectant parents.

I am still pretty worried about money. I'm a PhD student --- things are tight. I'm not worried about feeding you or housing you, thank goodness, but I do have a lot of feelings about not being able to take you on cool vacations, or not being able to sign you up for the same extracurriculars your peers are in. I want you to have _all_ the opportunities. I'm hoping I use these bad feelings to motivate myself, and grow as a person. I guess we will see. You will have to leave me some reviews so I can know how I did, raising you!

---

Your mom has been doing OK. She did have some bouts of severe back pain, to the point where she would wake up crying in the middle of the night (something you will also do, I'm sure). She blames most of this pain on dancing in heels. You should have seen her at the wedding we just went to: she danced for like 2 hours past her bed time!

On the topic of bed times, her's have sharply decreased to 7:30 pm. I have been trying to talk to you while I give her back rubs (can you hear me?) but I simply can't stomach going to bed so early. I have to hope you are learning my voice on road trips, when I sing in the car.

You have been kicking like a fiend! Just non-stop pa- pa- pow into your mom's ribs. The midwife said you will soon be too big to kick so violently, and that Emma will start to feel you rolling, or sweeping, instead. I think you have started to do that. You also had your first hiccuping fit. I am kinda sad that I don't get to feel all of this. I just feel you kick from time to time. I've asked Emma to keep me updated every time you do something in there, and so far she has been pretty good at it.

We've decided that this is the perfect emoji to describe what you are doing: 🫯.

Emma spends a lot of time poking and prodding at you. Almost like a baby discovering its own belly for the first time. I wonder if you can feel her, and if it's comforting for you.

See you soon!
