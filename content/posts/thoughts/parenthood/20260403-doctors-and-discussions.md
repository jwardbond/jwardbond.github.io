I am confused about who these posts are for. Are they for me to remember how I felt during this time in my life? Are they for you to read on your wedding day? Why not both? Are you "you" or are you "my kid"? am I Dad, Emma: Mom? (Wow, what a crazy shifting of identities is underway right now)

I think it makes more sense if I keep this as a diary. I am me, Emma is Emma, and you are our baby. If there are things I want to say to you, I will add them as footnotes or in their own section. This will be a diary of us raising our baby from lentil to adult (fingers crossed).

## The Serious Argument

This week was Emma's first pre-natal bloodwork. Everything looked good, except for WBC stuff. Pretty high. Not supposed to be concerned about that though.She has had to stop taking her allergy meds too, so maybe her immune system is acting up.

This first bloodwork spawned two conversations: the first was about how high her HGC was, and no wonder she has to pee 24/7.

The second: what do we do if tests come back and indicate physical or chromosomal abnormalities?

We fought about this latter question a bit. Raising a child with a disability is something I am very afraid of. I feel a bit selfish, but right now it's still my life, even if it slowly becoming more and more centered around our baby. I see, through my cousins, how much of a toll a sick kid can have on you. How much of your life it can consume. I also see how much love they have for those sick kids, and how much they would give if it made their kids' lives even a _little_ easier.

I guess the nature of the argument is this: Emma was raised (catholic) to believe in the nobility of personal struggle. That if you have a disabled child, that is your cross to bear, and you are a saint for bearing it. I was raised very liberally, in a pro-choice household[^1] and feel much less of a need to be a martyr.

I am not sure where we ended up in this argument. It's not like we made a list of criteria required for terminating the pregnancy, but it feels like we are on the same page now, and willing to get testing. Hopefully nothing is wrong, and this whole argument will just be an exercise in healthy communication.

## The Unserious Argument

We got a shit ton of baby books from the library. Too many to read, so we will just pick the ones that we enjoy and buy them. I am currently enjoying one called "crib sheet".

As I was reading these books, I realized that you don't put any weight on in the first trimester (usually). Given that, I wondered why I was jumping out of bed to make Emma midnight snacks. Baking cookies, etc. I made a comment to Emma along the lines of "Hey why am I bending over backwards to feed you here?", because I thought it was funny and felt like a guy with a feederism fetish. Emma interpreted it as a comment about her weight, which went over predictably poorly. I can see where she was coming from, but it was totally not my intention. I was in the dog house for a day and a half, and nothing I could say could make it better.
