That story about the midwife saying you drink you own pee... the one in the last post... that was actually something that happened recently. It just fit so much better in that paragraph I was already writing. Reality is the stories you tell yourself, but I guess this is my way of correcting the little bit of fiction I put out into the world.

The past month has been really busy for us.

We had a big lead scare. Emma got tested, and I checked all the pipes, got a water filter, sent samples of the tap water for testing, and put a plan in motion to clean up the soil in the backyard. It was hair-pullingly (if I had any) stressfull. But its all good now, Emma's levels were undetectable, and we have a good protocol to minimize any exposure from our flaky old shed.

I felt you kick for the first time. We were sitting in Scott and Theresa's backyard, enjoying a beautiful day in the sun. I had to sit with my hand on Emma's belly for a few minutes, and concentrate on the sensations in my hand, but _I felt it_: a teeny tiny little kick. Since then I have felt you kick numerous times, sometimes quite strongly. We like to joke that you are learning Muay Thai in the womb. You are now the size of a Barbie, so getting pretty big and strong!

We got the anatomy scan done. We can see you so clearly. I just want to "goochie goochie goo" your little nose!

I assembled your crib without instructions, and I only swore once. I felt like a true Dad after this. I hope you like it, and I hope you sleep super well in it.

...

The act of becoming a parent has made me start to reconsider the relationship I have with own parents. In particular, I've begun to imagine what it must feel like to raise a human to the point of independence.

It must be so _weird_. You spend so much time raising this totally helpless thing. The totality of your love and energy goes towards making your kid happy, enriched, and set up for success, and after doing that for almost **two decades** they slowly become independent from you, only to come back to you on their own sometime in their 30s. I can only imagine that it's like having a kitten, raising it, and then going "well, it's time to let it out into the woods, I hope it remembers me and visits every so often".

Even weirder is the fact that you raise your kid to be better than you, smarter, kinder, happier. Eventually the world around you changes, you get older, and suddenly your kid knows more than you (in some senses).

It's scary and freaky to think about, but I don't have to think about it for a while yet!

Love you pipes!
