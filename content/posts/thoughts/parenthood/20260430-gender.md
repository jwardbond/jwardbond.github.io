## Anxiety

I skipped writing something last week. I was having a rough, jaw-clenching sort of week. Some of this anxiety was external and out of my control[^1], which you think, by the simple act of recognition, would be largely ameliorated, but this has not been the case for me. On top of this baseload has been constant peaks of anxiety caused by my fear of being a failure. When I get a spare second at work, when I read about someone else being successful, when I sleep in too much, when I look at renting a bigger home and realize it is out of my budget. On my worst days, I feel like I have failed at being a productive member of society, and have personally let Emma and my baby down by not being wealthy, and by not having a clear career.

I thought writing this out would be cathartic, but it is not really feeling that way.

I am constantly told, as reassurance, that fatherhood will be measured in the love and time that I am able to provide. That millions (billions) of people around the world are also struggling---financially or mental health-wise or another way---and are raising healthy, happy children. It is still hard to internalize this message though, and I can't help but feel that with more money, or a bigger house, or a more focused life, I will be able to give my kid more of these opportunities.

It will work out in the end. It always does.[^2]

> Note to baby: By the time you are old enough to read this, I really wonder how you will see me as a person. Will you see me as the failure I sometimes feel like? I doubt it. I hope not. I hope you have seen nothing but the love and attention and time that I want to give you.

## Emma

This past week has also been a little rough on Emma. It seems like she has even less energy, if that is even possible, and that her emotions and cravings are getting worse. She isn't able to do as much muay thai, which sucks because this is a huge source of joy for her. Something I didn't expect: a lot her reluctance towards going to muay thai is not physical, but mental. She is proud of her fitness, and her ability to keep up with her class. She is embarassed to admit to other people that she can't perform at the same level as she did before. Yeah, part of the problem is that she can't yet tell people _why_ she is not as energetic, or can't take a body kick, but I get the sense that even when she _can_ tell people why, she will still be embarassed about being "less fit".

I get it, but I also feel like this is wild. I am so impressed with the amount she is exercising while pregnant, and I think the more pregnant she gets the more impressive any amount of activity is. Frick I can't imagine even going for a walk, let alone doing Muay Thai... and I know she will never stop doing Muay Thai! I'm really excited for her to be a kick-ass pregnant momma.

She is also talking about her body image a little more. She takes a lot of pride in being fit, and she is feeling slothful and overweight right now. Doesn't even matter that some of this weight is baby weight. It must be so scary to think about how much her body is going to change, and some of it is permanent! I need to constantly remind myself that a lot of the women around me have gone through this same thing, and came out the other end even happier. Hopefully Emma can remind herself of this as well.

## Gender

"It" is a giiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiiirl! Woohoo! This is what we both wanted. I have to be careful with my expectations, because I feel like things a kid will never work out the way you expect, but I have so many dreams for a daughter. I hope she's a daddy's girl, and that she loves going outside and looking at bugs and birds and plants with me. I keep picturing walking through a forest with her, both of us with little binoculars, looking at birds, and holding her hand while she babbles to me about all the things she is learning. I hope she rock climbs and skateboards and runs and sews and... Careful with my expectations!

> Note to baby: Man (woman) I am so curious about what kind of person you will be! What will you get from me? What will you get from your mom? I am going to make sure I tell you I love you every day, and give you lots of hugs.

After finding out we are having a daughter---which, again, is what I wanted---I got hit by a sudden feeling of anxiety. I have no idea how girls work! Emma is probably my best reference point. I don't remember Sierra as a teenage girl. What sort of things do I have to know to be a functional dad? What sort of things do I have to know to be a _great_ girl dad?

We weren't expecting to get the results so quickly (just one day after Steven and Natasha found out they were having a boy!). We didn't have a huge reveal ceremony or anything. We probably wouldn't have had one even if we were expecting to get the results back early. We did get Sarah to announce the gender to us on a phone call so we could screen record ourselves reacting, I don't think it was anything crazy.
