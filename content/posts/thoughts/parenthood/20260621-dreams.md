Man I don't know. I don't have a lot of introspective stuff to write about this week. I don't want to get out of the habit of writing, but we are just cruising lately. In some golden lull between the nausea-times and waddle-times. Or at least Emma is. I guess I am never subject to those endcaps.

Lately I've noticed Emma has been holding her belly a lot in bed. I think she's come to terms with the fact that you aren't just bloating anymore: you are pretty visible! I also saw her looking at her belly in the mirror and saying "I love my baby". She's pretty lucky she gets to carry you. We make sure we both tell you we love you before we sleep.

We had a midwife appointment this week. It's always a relief to hear that your heart is still kicking away. It keeps the anxiety away for a couple weeks, and helps us stay sane. (Insert anxiety graph) She told us that you are hiding behind the placenta, so we might not feel you kick for a while. Her and Emma could really hear you sloshing away in there though! I wonder what you are feeling and hearing. She also told us that you pee in, and drink, the same amniotic fluid (remind me to remind you about this when you are older).

...

Emma went to Australia a few years ago and brought back a stuffed wombat. A plush toy, not a taxidermied marsupial, despite what it's strange rigidity would have you believe. She intended to give it to her goddaughter, Clara, but for some reason grew pretty disgusted by the wombat and refused to pawn it off to an innocent child. She planned to donate him to a thrift store, but he spent enough time lingering at the edges of our bedroom, kicked around, and rolled under the bed, that he ended up wiath a name (Wombo) and personality (depressed but accepting, kinda like Eeyore). We couldn't get rid of him.

Emma maintained that poor Wombo was not long for this family --- an inmate perpetually on death row, only this time death row is the flourescently-lit aisles of a dusty value village. I maintained that Wombo would eventually be the favourite stuffed animal of our first child. I think I am winning this!

...

Emma has begun resting her hands on her belly all the time, unconciously. It's very cute.
