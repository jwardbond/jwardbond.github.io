Ok I am backdating this post a bit. So what. I don't always want to write in my diary. Sometimes it is enough to just hang on during a week, _without_ having to ruminate over it.

## Ultrasound

We got our first ultrasound this week. We were a little scared because Emma booked a clinic that had decidedly low reviews (it's the closest one, and she didn't really check). Some of these reviews were to the tune of "they told me my baby had no heartbeat but it actually did"... pretty egregious.

I almost panicked and booked at a private ultrasound clinic, but we decided to just go through with the poorly reviewed one. I had to wait outside while Emma went in. They called me in after about 15 minutes and I walked in to Emma giving me a thumbs-up with a big smile. We had successfully created a litlte gummy bear with a heartbeat! The old european lady promptly made me leave the room to finish some tests.

According to Emma, while I was waiting to be let in the ultrasound technician said

"You have a baby in there"

To which Emma replied

"But... is it alive"

"Yes, I wouldn't have told you you had a baby if it wasn't"

## Telling people

Once we knew our little raspberry had a heartbeat, we got to tell our closest friends and family. The first thing we did when we got home was call Shannon and Jade, Jade was kinda busy so it was a bit underwhelming, but Emma and Shannon cried again! I then tried to call my group chat, under the guise of talking about our upcoming greece trip. No one answered. Also underwhelming.

I just kinda sat in the living room beaming until someone finally got back to me. It was Steven, ultimately, which was perfect! We gossiped about our pregnant wives for a bit.

That evening my parents (Grumpy and Tutu) arrived to help build a catio, so we were pretty excited to tell them. I had this plan to sit down for dinner with everyone and set a _really_ tiny plate, with a little tiny spoon, and not touch it, and play it cool until someone asked about it.

My plans were stymied by both my parents going on zoom calls the second we got home for dinner. My mom was talking to Sierra, who was having a bad day, which was understandable. My dad, for some odd reason, thought it was the perfect time to start trying to troubleshoot my uncles internet speed.

Emma and I had to kill time for _45 minutes_.

Eventually we all managed to sit down, and the reveal went as planned. My mom was way more suprised than I expected. She started jumping up and down and spinning in circles. My dad was as you expect a dad to be. After we got done celebrating, I called Derek, and we all talked for a little bit.

Nothing felt really different over the next couple of days, except that I got called "dad" a few times by my parents. But my cats already call me that, so it wasn't so jarring.

The next people we told were Emma's parents. I have a video somewhere, which if someone is reading this and wants to watch, I will happily email. The gist of it was I tricked them into believing I was filming them trying chili oil, and then handed them the ultrasound. Theresa went _sprinting_ out of the room. Scott let out a kind of high pitched "eeeeeee!" It was pretty cute.

It turned out that the day before we went to Newmarket to tell them, she had already started putting a baby box together. I think we all want to believe that this was some sort of _moms know_ moment, but I am more inclined to believe that her friends had just been gassing her up about becoming a grandma soon. We told her not to tell anyone, and then the next day she told 3 people (we expected this).

Next we told Sierra, who was holding Henry, who promptly woke up. Nothing too exciting about that call. When Jacob got back from the beach, we told him too [^1]. One thing I have noticed is that no one is super surprised. I am not sure if this is because we are on the tail-end of our friends' baby boom, or if its because we have been telling so many people we wanted kids.

Lesson learned though. If you want people to be surprised, don't keep talking about "when I have kids..." etc.

## Genetic testing and worry

We talked a lot this week about what sort of genetic testing we will get, what do we want to test for, what happens if such-and-such a test is positive, etc. It was a tricky decision, but we ultimately opted for the lowest tier of testing: something that I am surprised I chose, given how paranoid I am about health.

Really it came down to a couple things:

The additional testing has a high false positive rate. Or rather, it has a low positive predictive value. It's pretty good at finding microdeletions, but abysmal at telling you if those microdeletions will have any impact. If it came back positive we would have to do an Amniocentesis or CVF, the former of which has a slight risk of inducing a miscarriage. These microdeletions are rare and hard to assess, so I figured I would spare us the possible additional testing. I felt like the risk of getting a false positive and then being sick with worry about it for 10 weeks, waiting for a test that carries a risk of miscarraige, was higher than the risk of missing a true positive that wouldn't be picked up in another test.

The biggest thing, though, was that I realized there are infinite things to be anxious about and, as far as I can tell, it's only going to get worse, so I'd better get better at handling it.
